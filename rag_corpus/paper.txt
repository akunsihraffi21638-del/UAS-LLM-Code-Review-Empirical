1. Introduction

    Modern software development is heavily based on collaborative workflows,
with Pull Requests (PR) serving as a central mechanism to integrate code
changes [1, 2, 3]. A PR goes through multiple phases, including submission,
review, change, and merging, each requiring coordination between contributors
and reviewers [1]. Effective PR management is crucial to maintaining software
quality, but it remains a bottleneck in many projects due to long review times,
inconsistent feedback, and inefficient communication [4]. Developers often expe-
rience delays due to unclear review comments, excessive revisions, and prolonged
waiting times before changes are accepted [5].

    Inefficiencies in the software development cycle, such as long review times,
can lead to frustration among team members, decreased productivity, and even
project delays [6]. As codebases become increasingly intricate, there is a growing
need for sophisticated automation tools to facilitate the code review process and
improve collaboration and teamwork [7]. In parallel, LLMs, such as Generative
Pre-trained Transformers (GPTs) [8] have rapidly evolved, demonstrating strong
capabilities in code generation, bug detection, and documentation assistance
[9, 10]. These models are capable of suggesting enhancements to existing code,
resolving unclear logical structures, and automating repetitive tasks, thereby
becoming indispensable tools for software engineers.

    Despite the increasing adoption of LLM-powered tools in development work-
flows, a systematic investigation into their impact on PR processes is lacking.
Previous research on the use of LLM in the PR context [9, 11, 12, 13] has focused
mainly on task-specific applications, such as commit message generation [14] and
documentation support [15], while neglecting to explore their phase-specific im-
pact across the entire PR lifecycle. Furthermore, while LLM-generated sugges-
tions can be beneficial, it is necessary to quantify their effectiveness in improving
review speed and reducing PR merge time.

    This study aims to fill this knowledge gap by empirically evaluating the
impact of GPT on code review waiting times, with a particular focus on phase-
specific effects. It examines their influence on resolution time, their role in dif-
ferent PR phases, and the reasons why contributors and reviewers adopt GPT as
assistance. Given the proven effectiveness of GPT on a wide range of software
engineering tasks, including code generation, debugging, and documentation,
understanding how and to what extent GPT enhances the effectiveness of PRs
in each phase is crucial to assess their usefulness in real-world operations. These
insights can guide the design of more efficient GPT-supported code review work-
flows and inform best practices for integrating AI assistance into collaborative
software development.

    Toward this goal, we examine the PRs that software developers actively
engaged with GPT to seek recommendations for code review tasks or problem-
solving. We refer to these as GPT-assisted PRs throughout the paper. In
contrast, GPT non-assisted PRs are those where developers either do not
use GPT at all or use it strictly for implementation tasks (i.e., making an API
call to a GPT model), without relying on it for code review insights or decision
making. Our research investigates how GPT-assisted PRs influence the duration
and quality of the code review process. For this purpose, we start by curating
a dataset of 25,473 PRs from 9,254 projects on GitHub. Next, we extract
six features (project age, no commits, files added, files deleted, files changed,
time to merge) to characterize projects, PRs, and code reviews. We then ana-
lyze how these factors influence the review and resolution times [16]. Finally, we
also investigate which development tasks involve the use of GPT-assisted PRs
throughout the PR phases, aiming to identify where such assistance is most
effectively integrated. In summary, our aim is to answer the following three
research questions:

RQ1:  Does GPT-assistance during the PR process reduce the resolu-
      tion time? We aim to understand the potential of LLMs to expedite
      the resolution of PRs in the code review process. Specifically, we analyze
      their impact on the total time required to resolve a PR, from its submis-
      sion to completion. Our results suggest a correlation between the use of
      GPT-assisted PRs and a decrease in merge time approximately from 23 to
      9 hours.

RQ2:  How does GPT assistance impact various phases of the PR pro-
      cess? We aim to further explore the effect of GPT in various phases of
      the PR process, such as submission, evaluation, modification, and waiting
      time, to determine where its support is most advantageous. Our analysis
      shows that GPT-assisted PRs complete faster across key phases of the re-
      view process. Specifically, they take 1 hour at the Review phase, 3 hours
      at Waiting for Changes, and 1 hour at Change, compared to 3 hours, 24
      hours, and 1 hour, respectively, for non-assisted PRs. These results high-
      light the potential of GPT to streamline the PR workflow, especially by
      reducing waiting times.

RQ3:  Why do contributors and reviewers use GPT during the PR
      process? We aim to better understand how programmers use GPT to
      enhance code quality, fix errors, and improve documentation. By analyz-
      ing these application patterns, we uncover how developers integrate this
      technology into their daily workflows, highlighting its most potential ben-
      efits. Our findings show that developers devote a significant part of their
      review time to refining and correcting the source code. Specifically, 60.26%
      of enhancement tasks occur during the Review phase, 42.31% during Wait-
      ing for Changes, and 35.90% during the Change phase. This explains the
      role of GPT as assistance in enhancing efficiency for those time-consuming
      activities.

2. Related Work

    In the following, we first review research on the challenges in code reviews
and collaboration before moving on to an overview of factors affecting PR life-
time and latency. Finally, we review studies on the application of LLMs in code
reviews.

Challenges in Modern Code Reviews (MCR) and Collaboration. Code
reviews are a critical aspect of modern software development, ensuring code
quality, maintainability, and adherence to best practices. However, the process
comes with several challenges that can affect efficiency, collaboration, and over-
all development workflow. For example, long waiting times where PRs often
remain unreviewed for extended periods, slowing down development. Kudr-
javets et al. [5] reported that between 29% and 63% of the overall code review
lifetime is spent waiting for the accepted changes to merge. Bacchelli and Bird
[3] reveal that scheduling and time issues also appeared challenging; yet, under-
standing the code and comments takes most of the review time. According to
these authors, code reviews tend to be less focused on detecting defects than ex-
pected and, instead, provide additional advantages, such as knowledge transfer,
increased team awareness, and creation of alternative solutions to problems.
Davila and Nunes [17] also reported that reviewers have found it difficult to

                                                   4
understand the code, which prompts the need for enhancements in visualizing
code changes to facilitate understanding. However, a recent study has shown
that ChatGPT is an alternative to generating some kind of explanations during
MCR [18]. Davila et al. [15] identified five challenges to adopting generative AI
in MCR: trustworthiness, lack of context, misleading review comments, security
and privacy, and token limits. Although prior studies have explored aspects of the
use of GPT in software development, they do not provide a systematic analysis
of its impact on waiting times and efficiency in all phases of the PR workflow.
Our study addresses this gap by focusing on its influence during specific code
review phases.

Factors Affecting PR Lifetime and Latency. Studying the factors that
influence PR latency is crucial for improving software development efficiency,
collaboration, and overall code quality. Understanding what causes delays in
PR review and merging can lead to optimizations that benefit both developers
and organizations [4], [19], [16], [20], and [21]. Zhang et al. [4] identified that the
number of commits at open time is negatively correlated with PR merging but
shifts to a positive correlation at close time. Rose [19] built a Multiple Linear
Regression Model (MLR) that indicates that the PR size has a statistically
significant effect on the review time, as the positive value of the regression
coefficient implies that as the PR size increases, the review time for PRs also
increases. Moreira Soares et al. [16] further emphasized that PRs possess some
structural characteristics, such as the number of modified files, the number of
commits, and the number of changed lines of code. Moreira Soares et al. [16]
concluded that the combination of structural characteristics may increase the
chances of PRs having a very short and long lifetime.

    Although prior research explores factors affecting PR latency (e.g., size, num-
ber of commits), there is limited research into how GPT can have an effect on
PR resolution time. Khatoonabadi et al. [20] developed a machine learning
model to predict the latency of the initial responses of the maintainers and con-
tributors after receiving the first feedback from the maintainer. These authors
highlighted that the timing of submissions and feedback are factors that have a
significant influence on response waiting times. Their approach, which provides
estimated waiting times, helps open-source projects collaborate better by allow-
ing maintainers and contributors to anticipate and address potential delays in
PR reviews. This study complements the aforementioned work by offering em-
pirical insights into GPT usage beyond commit messages, specifically examining
detailed time reductions in key phases such as review and change.

The Application of LLMs in Code Reviews. LLM-powered tools can offer
services designed to streamline various code review-related development tasks.
Research has been conducted on the impact of AI-driven generative tools in this
context [9], [14], [15], [13], [22], [23], [24], [25]. For instance, Davila et al. [15]
conducted a literature review on the application of AI-based generative tools
in a code review context and highlighted that AI-based tools can positively
impact code review efficiency by reducing reviewer workload and review duration
and increasing the likelihood of acceptance in GitHub projects. Xiao et al.

3. Study Setup

    In this paper, we investigate the impact of using GPT as an assistant tool
in the PR code review process. Specifically, we examine how GPT influences
the overall PR resolution time and how its effects vary between different phases
of the review workflow. We also aim to identify the types of review tasks for
which developers actively seek GPT support. Figure 1 presents an overview of
our study design. We begin with an automated procedure to identify potential
GPT-assisted PRs, followed by a semi-automated verification step to confirm
the actual use of GPT during the review. Finally, we map the verified GPT-
assisted PRs to their corresponding review phases. The remainder of this section
provides a detailed explanation of each step in our methodology.

3.1. Searching Potential GPT-assisted PRs
    To identify relevant PRs for our analysis, we rely on GitHub, which adopts

a PR-based approach to submit, review, and merge code contributions [17]. We
use the GitHub REST API [26] to retrieve a broad set of candidate PRs in
which the assistant of a GPT model could have been used during the review
process.

3.1.1. Searching
    Following a similar strategy to that of Tufano et al. [9], we conduct keyword-

based searches for mentions of "GPT" in four main PR fields.

    Project Name: Although some GPT-focused projects may not actually
       use GPT for PR reviews, we still record these mentions before filtering
       out irrelevant cases [27].

    PR Title: The use of GPT may be evident in the PR title (e.g., refactored
       using GPT suggestions [28]).

    Body+Comments: A PR description and/or comments can refer to
       GPT explicitly (e.g., code generated by GPT [29]).

    File(s) Name: References in filenames (e.g., chatgpt review.md [30]) can
       indicate GPT-generated artifacts.

3.1.2. Filtering
    To avoid projects with minimal activity or that lack meaningful collabora-

tion, we keep only projects that meet the following criteria: (1) the repository of
the PR has at least 10 stars [31], to remove toys or trivial projects. (2) at least
two contributors are involved in the review process, to exclude self-reviewed
PRs.

                                                   7
3.1.3. Downloading
    Based on the aforementioned criteria, a total of 25,473 pull requests were

collected from 9,254 repositories on May 9, 2024. For each PR, we extracted
detailed metadata, including the associated project name, URL, PR number
and title, event history, contributor information, creation date, comments, and
the set of modified files.

3.2. Identifying GPT-assisted PRs
    In this semi-automated step, our goal is to define the heuristics that indicate

the use of GPT to classify PRs as GPT-assisted or GPT-non-assisted. By doing
this, we exclude false positives (i.e., PRs that mentioned GPT as discussed in
Section 3.1 but did not use the GPT model to assist in the code review process).
This step ensures that the PRs used for our analysis are PRs where GPT models
are used in the code review process, rather than just being part of the project
code.

3.2.1. Random Samples
    With the goal of discovering patterns related to the use of GPT in PRs, we

randomly sampled a total of 450 GitHub PRs to examine indicators of GPT
assistance in code review activities. Three authors independently inspected 150
unique PRs in three separate rounds, analyzing project names, PR titles, mod-
ified files, and PR bodies. In Round 1, we reviewed the first set of 150 PRs
and cross-validated our observations to ensure that the identified patterns con-
sistently reflected GPT-assisted contributions. In Rounds 2 and 3, we applied
the same procedure to two additional sets of 150 PRs each. After each round,
the reviewers met to consolidate recurring patterns, clarify ambiguous cases,
and resolve classification disagreements through discussion until consensus was
reached. The findings of Round 1 are presented in Table 1. In particular, most
PRs were ultimately classified as GPT-non-assisted, as GPT was often inte-
grated into the software (e.g., through GPT APIs) but not explicitly used to
support the code review itself.

3.2.2. Defining Heuristics
    We defined four heuristics to analyze and label the presence of GPT-assisted

PRs, as outlined in Table 2. These heuristics are reinforced by a regular
expression-based technique (detailed in the appendix), which automatically clas-
sifies the collected PR instances as either GPT-assisted or GPT-non-assisted
based on the identified patterns: H1: Project name, H2: PR title, H3: Modi-
fied files, H4: PR body+comments.

n-gram to determine which configurations offered the most valuable insights
for detecting recurring patterns. In contrast to the methodology employed by
Tufano et al. [9], which used 2- or 3-grams, we found that a maximum size

3.2.4. Inspection
    To ensure the reliability of our heuristics, two authors independently ex-

amined 200 randomly chosen PRs by carefully reviewing the labels generated
during the execution of the heuristics. They then discussed unambiguous cases
where the labeling was unclear to reach an agreement, such as whether the PR
used GPT as an assistant. In the final step, the heuristics were adjusted and
reapplied to improve accuracy and consistency. We performed three rounds
of refinement/reapply until we obtained an accuracy of 95% as an acceptable
agreement score in the labeling of PRs.

the review process, we excluded PRs in which the only participants, either as

computing the distances between factors), we finally test the differences between
the PRs supported by or without GPT by getting the minimum distance value
from the Manhattan distance.

4. Results

4.1. Does GPT-assistance during the PR process reduce the resolution
      time?

Motivation: Recent studies have investigated the influence and popularity of
ChatGPT on code review [37], [11], [24], [38]. The potential impact of using
GPT models as assistants throughout the entire PR lifecycle remains largely un-
explored. This research question investigates whether relying on GPT-assisted
PRs affects code review resolution time in GitHub projects by analyzing the
impact of GPT models on the overall merge time of PRs.

Approach: To answer this research question, we build a Multiple Linear Re-
gression Model (MLRM) [39] to empirically explain PR lifetime. For this pur-
pose, we examine previous studies to identify a list of known factors that sig-
nificantly influence PR resolution time [4], [16], [19], [40]. Bernardo et al. [41]
defines resolution time as the time it takes for a code change to be merged into
the development branch after review. This implies that the change is not fully

4.3. Why do contributors and reviewers use GPT during the PR pro-
      cess?

Motivation: Our prior analysis revealed that the use of GPT was associated
with notable variations in the duration of individual PR phases, suggesting
that its influence extends beyond the overall resolution time to phase-specific
developer activities. While earlier studies have examined how developers use
GPT for specific code review tasks [9, 12], recent work has raised concerns about
its influence on review discussions and developer responses [24, 38]. Although
GPT tools show promise across various software engineering activities, little is
known about their role across different phases of the PR workflow. This suggests
an underexplored but potentially broad impact of GPT-based tools on the code
review process. To fill the gap, we analyze the purposes for which developers
use GPT within GitHub PR phases.

Approach: To explore the purposes for which developers use GPT as assis-
tance, we performed a detailed analysis of a statistical sample of 310 randomly
selected GPT-assisted PRs drawn from the dataset described in Section 3.3.
Initially, two authors independently examined and assigned labels to each in-
stance (e.g., enhancement, documentation, fixing bugs). The labeling process

                                                  16
was conducted in two rounds, each covering about 155 GPT-assisted PRs. Af-
ter each round, the authors met to refine the labels, and the final classification
was based on the taxonomy proposed by Tufano et al. [9]. The analysis specif-
ically targeted events in which developers explicitly recorded their prompts to
GPT and the corresponding suggestions generated by the model. This classifi-
cation enabled a structured understanding of the functional contexts in which
GPT-assisted PRs are integrated into the PR workflow.

6. Threats to Validity

    In this section, we discuss the threats to the internal and external validity
of our study.

6.1. Construct Validity

Identifying GPT Use via Explicit Mentions: the use of a single keyword
("GPT") to identify PRs that potentially involve the assistance from ChatGPT.
This keyword-based method may result in false negatives, where GPT assistance
was used but not explicitly mentioned, as well as false positives, where the term
"GPT" appears in a different context (e.g., in a discussion). Consequently, this
introduces potential bias in the classification of PRs as GPT-assisted and may
affect the accuracy of our findings regarding the actual impact of GPT use on
the review process. While a semi-automated verification step helps mitigate this
issue, future work could benefit from more robust detection methods, such as
NLP-based intent analysis.

Definition of GPT as Assistance: A potential threat lies in how the GPT
assistance is operationally defined. This study relies on heuristics to label PRs as
GPT-assisted or non-assisted, but these heuristics may inadvertently misclassify
instances, particularly when contributors do not explicitly document their use
of GPT in PR titles, comments, or files. To mitigate this, manual inspections
were performed to refine the heuristics and achieve high labeling precision (95%).
However, some misclassification may persist.

Measurement of PR Phases: The study measures the duration of PR phases
(e.g., review, waiting) using timestamps and events extracted from GitHub.
While these metrics provide an objective basis for analysis, they may not fully
capture qualitative aspects, such as the complexity of interactions or the nature
of GPT's contributions. In addition, inconsistencies in how timestamps and
events are logged across projects could introduce variability. Efforts to mitigate

                                                  20
these issues include cross-checking phase definitions and excluding PRs with
missing or illogical events and timestamps.

Impact Attribution: Attributing observed reductions in PR phase times to
GPT assistance alone is challenging. Other factors, such as task simplicity or
team dynamics, could contribute to the observed effects. The use of a linear
model helps account for some confounding variables (e.g., project age, number
of commits), but residual confounding remains a potential threat. By addressing
these threats, the study aims to provide a robust assessment of GPT's influence
on PR phases. However, future research could refine the constructs further, for
example, by incorporating direct user feedback on their interactions with GPT
or using more sophisticated techniques to distinguish GPT contributions from
other factors.

Interactions with GPT: The nature of developer interactions with GPT may
not be fully captured by simple time measurements. For example, the impact
of GPT on developer behavior (e.g., whether it encourages more efficient cod-
ing practices or simply accelerates repetitive tasks) is not directly measured in
this study. Future research could incorporate qualitative data, such as devel-
oper surveys or interviews, to better understand the cognitive and collaborative
effects of GPT assistance on the code review process.

6.2. Internal Validity

Confounding Variables: The impact of GPT on PR resolution times may be
influenced by factors other than GPT assistance, such as the complexity of the
code changes, the experience of the developer, or the team's workflow dynamics.
While the study controls for some confounding variables through regression
models, residual effects may still persist. Further steps, such as matching or
propensity score analysis, could help control for additional confounders that
were not included in the model.

Task Categorization: Categorizing the tasks for which GPT is used could be
subjective and may vary depending on the developer's interpretation of GPT's
assistance. If task categorization is inconsistent or unclear, it could skew the
analysis and affect the conclusions drawn about the types of assistance most
commonly used.

The Role of the Linear Model in Time Reduction: While the study finds
a reduction in time for various phases of the code review process when GPT
is used, it is important to ensure that this reduction in time is genuinely due
to GPT's assistance and not influenced by other factors (e.g., more efficient
developers or less complex PRs). If time reduction is attributed to factors other
than GPT, it could undermine the internal validity of the study.

6.3. External Validity

Platform-Specific Bias: This study analyzes GitHub data, which may have
unique characteristics that limit the generalizability of findings to other plat-
forms such as GitLab or Bitbucket. For instance, GitHub as a large open-source

                                                  21
community and due to its widespread usage may introduce dynamics that are
not presented in smaller or private repositories. Future research could include
multiple platforms to better assess the broader applicability of these findings.

Repository Selection: The data included in this study were selected based
on a threshold of 10 stars, indicating a relatively active user base. While this
ensures that the results reflect commonly used, publicly available code, it also
introduces selection bias. The results may not generalize well to less popular
or private repositories, where developer practices may differ. Expanding the
dataset to include a wider range of repositories, including those with lower
visibility or fewer contributors, would help assess the external validity of the
findings.

Task Variability: PRs vary widely in terms of content, including code size,
task complexity, and the nature of the proposed changes. Although we at-
tempted to control for these factors, the diversity in PRs introduces a level of
variability that could influence the generalizability of the results. To address
this, future studies could consider stratified sampling or clustering based on
PR characteristics to assess the impact of GPT more accurately across different
types of tasks.

7. Conclusion

    This study highlights the potential of generative AI tools, particularly large
language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, to enhance collaborative software
development. Through an analysis of their application in GitHub pull requests
(PRs), we observe substantial improvements across key phases of the PR work-
flow, most notably within the review phase, which exhibits a 33% reduction in
review duration and an 87% decrease in waiting time for acceptance. These
results underscore the capacity of GPT to improve code review efficiency and
support diverse tasks, including code enhancement, bug resolution, and doc-
umentation generation, with the most significant gains occurring during the
review process. While the contributions of GPT are clearly beneficial in cer-
tain phases, its limited influence during the submission and post-acceptance
phases points to areas for further development and optimization. Future re-
search should investigate how GPT can be more effectively integrated across
the entire PR lifecycle and evaluate its impact on long-term productivity. As
LLMs continue to advance, they are poised to become essential components
of software engineering, promoting greater efficiency, collaboration, and code
quality. By harnessing the capabilities of these tools, both practitioners and
researchers can shape the evolution of collaborative development workflows and
drive innovation in the field of software engineering.
